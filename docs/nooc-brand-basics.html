What Nooc is

Nooc lets people reserve a seat by the hour in calm, professional public spaces — cafés, hotel lobbies, and similar venues. You book a specific seat for a specific window, show up, and work.

Two things follow from that, and they should shape anything you make. First, the product is about calm and focus, not hustle or productivity-hacking. Second, it is about real, specific places — a named café on a named street, not a generic co-working stock photo.

The official one-liner: “Reserve calm workspaces by the hour.”
Longer form, used as the site description: “Reserve a seat by the hour in calm, professional public environments.”

Color

One green, one cream, and not much else

Nooc has a single brand color. There is no secondary brand hue — no orange accent, no gradient. Restraint is the point, so resist adding a second color to make a layout work.

One thing to know

The green inside the current logo files is #274229 — slightly more olive than the #0F5132 brand green. The product code was recently unified on #0F5132, but the logo artwork hasn't been redrawn yet.

For new assets, use #0F5132. If you place the existing logo file next to a #0F5132 field, the two greens will not quite match — so either put the logo on cream or on the deep green, or ask for the mark to be re-supplied in the brand green.

Logo

A cream cup with two curls of steam

The mark is a wide, conical cup tapering to a point, with two ribbons of steam rising from it. It reads as coffee and as a small sheltered place — a nook. It is always solid cream on a solid green field, and it appears both on its own and in a vertical lockup above the wordmark.

Previews below are the actual files. The first two are opaque rectangles, so what you see — including the tile of green behind the mark — is exactly what the file contains.

Important — please read before you start

There is no vector version of the logo. No SVG, no AI, no EPS. Every file that exists is a raster PNG, and the largest is 1024 × 1024 px.

In practice: do not use the logo above about 900 px wide, and do not put it in print at any meaningful size — it will soften. If you need it large or in print, ask for a vector redraw before you begin, because it will need to be drawn from scratch rather than traced cleanly.

Only the vertical lockup has a transparent background. The other files are solid rectangles, so you cannot drop them onto a photo or a colored field without the tile showing.

Using it

Do

  • Keep clear space around the mark equal to at least the height of one steam curl.
  • Use the transparent lockup when you need the mark over a color or image.
  • Keep the mark cream on green — that contrast is the identity.
  • Let it sit small and quiet. The product uses it at 28–48 px in most places.

Don't

  • Don't scale the raster files past ~900 px or use them in print.
  • Don't recolor the mark, add gradients, outlines or shadows.
  • Don't rotate it, stretch it, or separate the steam from the cup.
  • Don't place the opaque green tile on another green — the two greens will clash.
  • Don't rebuild the wordmark by typing “Nooc” in Inter. It isn't Inter, and the real typeface isn't documented.

Typography

Inter, worked hard

Nooc uses one typeface for everything: Inter. It's free from Google Fonts. All the personality comes from weight and spacing rather than from mixing families, so don't introduce a second face.

Display — 900 black, tracking −0.045em
Find a Nooc
Heading — 700 bold, tracking −0.025em
Reserve a workspace by the hour
Body — 400 regular, tracking −0.01em
Discover nearby cafés and workspaces. Book a spot in seconds and get focused.
UI / buttons — 600 semibold
Check availability
Label — 600 semibold, uppercase, tracking +0.1em
Reserve seats individually by the hour

The two rules that matter

Big type gets tighter. Headlines are set heavy — 700 to 900 — with negative letter-spacing, roughly −0.025em for headings and −0.045em at the largest display sizes. This is what makes Nooc's headlines look like Nooc.

Small type gets looser. Anything small and uppercase — eyebrows, labels, badges, nav — opens up to +0.1em or more. The product runs these at 10–11 px.

Weights actually in use, in order of frequency: 500 medium (most text), 700 bold and 600 semibold (headings, buttons), 900 black (large display only). Turn on Inter's stylistic set ss01 if your tool exposes it — the product does, which gives a single-storey a.

Shape & feel

Soft corners, soft shadows, plenty of air

Nooc's shape language is rounder than most software. Corners are generous, pills and circles are everywhere, and shadows are unusually faint.

12pxsmall controls
16pxbuttons, cards
24pxpanels
40pxlarge sheets
fullpills, badges
  • Shadows are barely there. The house shadow is about 5% black, blurred wide — closer to a soft lift than a drop shadow. If your shadow reads as grey, it's too strong.
  • Cards are white on warm paper — pure #FFFFFF panels floating on #FBFAF9. That slight warmth difference is what separates them, more than any border.
  • Small caps do the labeling. Tiny uppercase letter-spaced text is used constantly for structure. It's a signature — lean on it.
  • Photography is real venues. Interiors of actual cafés and lobbies, natural light, no people posing at laptops. If you need imagery, ask for venue photos from the product rather than sourcing stock.
Voice

Plain, warm, and specific

Nooc's copy is short and unhurried. It says what happens, without exclamation marks or growth-marketing energy. Real lines from the product:

Find your perfect nooc

welcome screen

Discover nearby cafés and workspaces. Book a spot in seconds and get focused.

welcome screen

No locations in this area yet. Feel free to search a different spot on the map.

empty state

Thanks so much for reserving a spot with Nooc, we hope you had a great experience.

follow-up email

Thanks again for being an early part of this. We're excited to keep building.

follow-up email

Sounds like Nooc

  • Sentence case, almost always.
  • Verbs that describe the actual action: reserve, book, find, search.
  • Honest about being early — “Nooc is still growing” appears in real emails.
  • “a nooc” is used as a common noun for a spot. Lowercase.

Doesn't

  • No exclamation marks, no “Unlock,” “Supercharge,” “Level up.”
  • No hustle framing — it's about focus and calm, not output.
  • Not “co-working.” Nooc is seats in ordinary public venues.
  • Don't promise coverage. The venue network is small and growing.

Worth asking for before you begin

  • A vector logo. Nothing exists above 1024 px. Anything for print, large-format, or animation needs the mark redrawn first.
  • The logo in #0F5132. Current files are the older #274229 green.
  • A horizontal lockup, if your layout needs one. Only the vertical mark-above-wordmark version exists.
  • The wordmark's typeface. It isn't Inter and isn't documented anywhere — if you need to set “Nooc” as type, someone has to identify or redraw it.
  • Venue photography, rather than sourcing stock interiors.
